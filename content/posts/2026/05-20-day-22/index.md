Today has been both extremely long and extremely "hilly".  I'm sitting in the
Auberge de Jeunesse in Grenoble. It's the second time I've been here. The
first time was in 2010 and that was the first ever time I'd stayed in a
hostel. So here I am 16 years later. It hasn't changed. I have a beer and a
pizza which the kind English lady at the reception supplied for me "put your
bike in the bike locker and don't leave it unattended. Last time somebody did
that it was stolen!". I'm in a crowded 8 bed dormitory.

My decision to cycle 110 miles today was triggered by not being able to find
affordable accomodation on the route. There was affordable accomodation in
Briancon but Briancon was only 50ish miles away and I saw there was an Auberge
de Jeunesse in Grenoble for £25 and it only required me to cycle two cols: Col
de Mont Genevre and Col de Lauteret. It would be a hard effort but I had until
10PM to check-in to the hostel.

I was happily cycling along and it had alread been almost 3 hours and I was
hungry. The host at the Bed and Breakfast had provided a very good breakfast
for the guests and went so far as to engage in polite conversation. She didn't
speak English very well but she tried and was good natured and it was a good
accomodation and I left with a full stomach and a cheese sandwich. It wasn't a
good cheese sandwich.

![Camera/IMG_20260520_113623_978.jpg](202605191841-mountainsw.jpg)
_Climbing up and up and up_

I arrived at Bardonecchia and the village was alive enough and there was a
supermarket and I found a pizza and a cola and, thank the gods, some sweets
without gelatine. This made me happy. It was already lunchtime so I sat down
and monged my pizza slice and consumed my cola on a bench and found a water
fountain (they are everywhere) to fill my water bottle and **I continued to
climb**.

![Camera/IMG_20260520_122229_775.jpg](202605191841-snacktime.jpg)
_Snack Time_

I continued to climb but I noticed that there was less traffic, and also no
signs of any upcoming cols. It felt strange. I checked my map - I was
expecting to find "Col de Mont Genevre" but I found **Col de Mauvais Pas**.
This was not the col I was looking for. But would it do?

Searched the internet did I to no avail. The "AI" result said it was closed
but provided some vague reason and none of the references backed it up. I
passed a couple that were pulling onto the road with bicycles and nodded at
them. I then turned back "Do you speak English?" they looked relieved. They
_did_ speak English and they were from the Netherlands. They informed me that
the col was closed, or at least cars couldn't pass it and a motorbike turned
back, apparently. "Oh dear" I said. I was realising that the past (one or
two?) hours had been in vein - the extent of which I confirmed to be half of
my ride so far. Much pointless ascending. "It is a long way to go back" the
Netherlandese informed me. "It was" I smiled.

> The Col du "Mauvais Pas" translates to "Wrong Step". Which was appropriate.
> Never trust Stava routing (although part of me wonders in retrospect if it
> would have been passable).

Going back didn't feel bad. It would add to the mileage but I still had time - or did I?
It took 26 minutes to _descend at speed_ to the point where I could continue
to Col de Mont Genevre and by that time it was already past 1PM. I then had
100 miles/160k to go and 9 hours to get there. Most of the rest of the day
would be spent in trying to figure out if I would miss the hostel check-in.

There would be no time to stop and stare. But I did stop and take photographs.

![Camera/IMG_20260520_143505_536.jpg](202605191841-downwards.jpg)

I was on the _bonne route_ and climbing. I had used my Pink Floyd bonus on the
first ascent. I tried to listen to it again but it didn't have the same
cathartic effect and my OpenShockBoneConductingHeadphones were running out of
battery.

There were lots of tunnels. I was riding mindlessly when I saw a sign with a
red circle and in the circle was a bicycle. I wondered "does that mean
**beware** of bicycles or that **bicycles are prohibited**. Then I saw a
1km tunnel _probably it means no cycles in the tunnel_. Conveniently the course
didn't go through the tunnel but by a side-road that said "authorized vehicles
only". I guess Strava authorized me.

It took me through some spooky, crumbling, abandonned tunnels. It reminded me
of Lord of the Rings when Gandalf had to take the adventurers through the
Dwarven mines.

I heard a sonorous voice reverberating, coming towards me. It didn't sound
like a ghost and it was another cyclist clearly testing his voice against the
reverberation of the tunnel "Ciao!" he said (he didn't sing after that). I
tested my voice "Whooo!".

The road featured some holes in the mountainside "🏡100m this way" it said,
implying that you'd go into the cave for 100m to find refuge. I wondered if it
was a trap. Some of them seemed to be more directly accessible.

It was now 16:00 and I had about 110k to go. An entire, acceptable, days ride
at around the time I'd normally finish. I should've left the accomodation
earlier. I did my calculations and with another 1500m or so of ascent it was
possible but the margin was _thin_. If I had a puncture, or stopped too long
I'd miss the check-in.

What was sure was that I would need some calories. I stopped at the first
bakery in France there were two sandwiches "Est-ce que vous avez une sandwich
vegiterienne?" "Oui" she said and said what it was and I didn't understand
"c'est quoi?" "goats cheese and honey" she said in English without joy. But it was a
sizeable sandwich and I was very happy to eat it.

Ugly thoughts in my head: "oh wow look how far I'll ride today,
aren't I good" - that perculiar satisfaction of making an larger than normal effort.
