I woke this morning in Caen after sleeping with no sheets in the smelly oven that was the
hostel dormitory. Left at around 6:45am and cycled 15k or so to Ouistream for
the ferry and was promptly boarded and was able to get breakfast. 6 hours
were spent reading, working and dozing. The train in the UK was cancelled
(unsurprisingly) but I was able to take a longer route back arriving in
my hometown at around 17:00 UK time.

That's the end. I've:

- Been on the road for 29 days which included 2 rest days (Oberstdorf and Rovereto) and 4 days in Verona.
- Ridden 2,516 kilometers (1,563 miles) horizontally and 25.54  (15.8 miles) kilometers vertically.
- Written 25 blog posts.
- Visited 8 countries (France, Belgium, Netherlands, Luxembourg, Germany, Switzerland, Austria and Italy).
- Gave one conference talk.
- Lost around 4KG of weight.

## What Worked

- The Bike and the Bags (mostly): everything stayed together
- The [Open Run](https://uk.shokz.com/products/openrun) headphones. Massive
 improvement. No ear damage, can hear things going on, good controls, quick
 charging and most importantly, they basically **just work**.
- The [Patagonia Convertible
  Pants](https://eu.patagonia.com/gb/en/product/mens-quandary-convertible-hiking-pants/55257.html).
  Maybe you can buy something siilar from Decathlon but these have quality and
  good pockets while looking respectable as either shorts of pants.
- [Gramin Fenix 7](https://www.garmin.com/en-GB/p/735520/) smart watch. While
  not really essential for a **bicycle tour** it provides some mapping
  redundancy, music redundancy, and a **very handy** bright torch. The battery
  also lasts weeks when not using GPX etc. On the negative side it is a chunky
  and expensive thing to have on your wrist but you get used to it.
- **Strava Routing**: Using strave to plan the routes each day was largely a
  success. They were good 99% of the time (if you ignore it taking me up
  closed mountain passes and directing me through dense forest).
- **Decent Tyre Levers**: In October the tyres were new and it was a bloody
  affair to remove the inner tube. Probably the tyres were looser this time,
  but having some very sturdy tyre levers certainly helped.

## What Was Kinda Working

- **The laptop**: while the laptop survived and only fell off once I had to be
  careful when riding trails to avoid potholes and other obstacles that could
  cause it to get popped off. It would be **nice** to have a more stable way
  of carrying the laptop, but this way worked well enough.
- **Exporting GPX tracks from Garmin Connect for the blog**: it's possible but
  only via. a workaround due to the webapp not letting you download large GPX
  files. If I accidentally stopped the course I have to manually stich the GPX
  tracks together.

## What Didn't

- **Camping**: For whatever reason sleep and the cold kept me awake. Maybe I
  need a better pillow solution (I'm using the sleeping bag stuff bag packed
  with clothes). In general I was warm enough with my thermals but still
  uncomfortable. Maybe I'm getting old.
- **My Italian**: kindof improved a little bit for the week/s I was in Italy.
  But having some knowledge of Spanish and being reasonable in French it seems
  like a low-hanging fruit to improve my Italian.
- **Internal cable routing**: having the gear cable break meant a bike shop
  visit rather than me simply replacing the cable myself. It may be possible
  for me to do it, but it on the road but it might not be easy...
